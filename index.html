taBtaP

An iPhone keypad that sits next to your keyboard

Stand your iPhone next to your keyboard and it becomes a second keyboard for your Mac. Tap a button and an app comes to the front, a shortcut gets pressed, or the volume changes.

Download the Mac Agent Free · macOS 14 or later · Version 1.0 beta · 1.3 MB

The iPhone app is coming to the App Store soon.

Buttons instead of ⌘Tab

Put the apps you bounce between — Chrome, Slack, Figma — on buttons. Tap one and that app comes straight to the front. If it's already in front, you can choose to hide it or jump to its next window.

Press and hold a button to see that app's open windows. You can also turn a window you use a lot into its own button.

Record a shortcut, done

Pick a button on your Mac and press the keys — that's it. A combo like ⌘⇧K can be pressed once, chained with others in a sequence, or held down for as long as you hold the button.

Shortcuts work the same with non-Latin input sources like Korean or Japanese.

Volume and monitor brightness

Tap a volume or brightness button to open a slider. External monitors work too if they support DDC/CI.

Edit buttons on your Mac

Open Edit Buttons from taBtaP in the menu bar to add and arrange buttons. Changes show up on your iPhone right away. Eight buttons per page; swipe for more pages.

A direct connection on your Wi-Fi

No servers in between. The connection is encrypted with a key created when you pair, and the iPhone only ever says "button 3 was pressed." Your Mac only runs actions you set up yourself.

Install

  1. Open the DMG and drag taBtaP to your Applications folder. The app is notarized by Apple.
  2. Open taBtaP. An icon appears in the menu bar, and it starts automatically when you log in.
  3. Allow Accessibility access, which window switching and shortcuts need. The menu bar icon walks you through it.
  4. Choose Connect iPhone in the menu bar, then scan the QR code or enter the 6-digit code in the iPhone app.

macOS 14 Sonoma or later (Apple silicon or Intel), iOS 17 or later, both on the same Wi-Fi.

Price

The Mac Agent is free. The iPhone app is free too, with no ads for the first week after you install it. After that it shows ads.

FAQ

Why does it need Accessibility access?

macOS requires it to read other apps' windows, switch between them, and press keys on your behalf. taBtaP doesn't record your screen or what you type.

A shortcut button doesn't do anything.

Check that Accessibility access is on. While the cursor is in a password field, macOS turns on Secure Input and blocks simulated keys.

External monitor brightness doesn't change.

Try turning on DDC/CI in your monitor's menu. Some models and connections (hubs, some USB-C docks) don't support it.

How do I uninstall it?

Quit taBtaP from the menu bar and move it from Applications to the Trash. Your buttons are kept in ~/Library/Application Support/TabTap.
